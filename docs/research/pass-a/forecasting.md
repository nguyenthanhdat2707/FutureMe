### Evidence labels used in this audit

- **Confirmed:** directly supported by the current product contracts or by the cited source for the narrow claim made.
- **Inferred:** a defensible interpretation, but not yet established by Future Me data.
- **Unknown:** Future Me currently lacks an operational definition, resolved outcomes, or empirical comparison.
- **Rejected:** the claim is contradicted by the contracts, confuses a proxy with the target, or exceeds the cited evidence.

### Measurement labels

- **Latent:** a construct not directly observed, such as subjective capacity or focus propensity.
- **Direct observable:** an event or quantity whose occurrence can be recorded without inferring a mental state, subject to source validity.
- **Proxy:** an observable correlated with a target in some contexts but not identical to it.
- **Weak label:** a noisy operational label, often generated by a heuristic or an action whose semantics are ambiguous.
- **User report:** an explicit report of experience or outcome. This may be the correct measurement of a subjective construct, but it can still contain recall, framing, and missingness error.
- **Outcome:** the prospectively defined event or value used to resolve a forecast.

### Measurability decisions

- **Measurable now:** the existing contracts and inputs can resolve the narrowly defined target without a new feedback mechanism.
- **Measurable with feedback:** the target can be resolved if a specific, timestamped question or verified event is added and missing outcomes remain unknown.
- **Approximable:** a distribution or indicator can be estimated from defined proxies, but it must not be represented as the latent construct itself.
- **Not defensible:** no current operationalization supports the named claim, or the label would collapse plans/proxies/inferences into truth.

## 2. Contract facts that constrain forecasting

1. **Personal states are beliefs, not objective truths.** `OVERLOADED`, `DISRUPTED`, `FLOW`, and related states are explicitly temporary system interpretations in the domain contract.
2. **Plans are not observations.** Calendar entries and task records describe intentions or commitments unless separately observed or confirmed.
3. **Forecasts are optional for the MVP path.** The MVP may show a simple forecast, but the core decision-support flow must work without one. Numerical forecasting is not a required dependency.
4. **Current feedback is coarse.** The end-of-block flow supports `Completed`, `Partially completed`, `Didn't happen`, or `Skip`, plus optional reasons such as `Low energy` or `Interrupted`. It does not currently collect validated focus minutes, momentary energy, or continuous behavior.
5. **The architecture contract says there is no MVP analytics/tracking.** Therefore, claims that assume a ready stream of device telemetry or a large historical training set are outside the current evidence boundary.
6. **Unknown and silence are valid.** Skipped feedback, absent telemetry, and an empty calendar interval cannot be silently scored as failure, success, inactivity, or free cognitive capacity.
7. **The trust contract separates** what was planned, observed, inferred, confirmed, predicted, and eventually happened. Any target definition that erases those distinctions is invalid for this product.

These facts favor resolvable, short-horizon event targets and deterministic constraint checks. They do not support a present-tense claim that Future Me already knows a user's latent capacity, focus, energy, or overload.

### Ground-truth conclusions

- The strongest near-term labels are **specific completion outcomes** and **specific observed context changes**.
- **Deadline miss** is a resolvable event, but **deadline risk** becomes calibrated only across a collection of comparable, prospectively logged forecasts.
- **Clock-time feasibility** is deterministic arithmetic, not personal-capacity forecasting.
- **Focus, energy, usable capacity, and subjective overload** are not interchangeable. Each requires its own construct and observation model.
- A user report is not automatically a weak substitute for “objective truth.” For subjective energy or experienced focus, the report may be the target measurement. It is still subject to question wording, timing, nonresponse, and reactivity.
- Device activity, calendar free time, task checkboxes, and session length are never ground truth for focus or productivity merely because they are machine-recorded.

Independent evidence supports the caution. Knowledge workers describe productivity using multiple dimensions beyond app-use time ([Kim et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/understanding-personal-productivity-how-knowledge-workers-define-evaluate-and-reflect-on-their-productivity/)). Focus measurement using thought probes varies substantially with probe wording and method ([Weinstein 2018](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-017-0891-9)), and probes can themselves change the phenomenon measured ([Greve & Was 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2022.103355)). A validated energetic-arousal scale shows that subjective “energy” can be operationalized, but as a self-rated state rather than a direct measure of work capacity ([Thayer 1986](https://doi.org/10.2466/pr0.1986.58.2.607)). Repeated momentary self-report also creates selective missingness: compliance patterns may be non-random ([Courvoisier et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026733)). None of these studies validates a Future Me focus, energy, or capacity score.

## 4. Structured claim records

### FA-01 — Forecast only targets that can later be resolved

- **Claim:** Every scored forecast needs a fixed target, reference entity, horizon, resolution time, and outcome rule written before the outcome is known.
- **Status:** **Confirmed** as a methodological requirement and consistent with the product trust contract.
- **Repository evidence:** Plans, observations, forecasts, outcomes, and feedback are separate domain objects; silence and unknown are valid.
- **Independent evidence:** Proper scoring rules evaluate a predictive distribution against the event/value that materializes ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)). The Brier score is defined for repeated probability forecasts of resolved events ([Brier 1950](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%281950%29078%3C0001%3AVOFEIT%3E2.0.CO%3B2)).
- **Negative evidence:** “Recommendation confidence,” “deadline pressure,” and generic “capacity” lack an automatic resolution event. A recommendation can be reasonable even when the chosen outcome is bad, and vice versa.
- **Assumptions:** Outcome definitions remain stable; resolution uses only information unavailable at forecast time; unresolved cases are retained.
- **Failure conditions:** Retroactive target changes, silent label imputation, duplicated forecasts for the same episode without a declared evaluation rule, or scoring selective user responses as if missing at random.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for the evaluation principle; **unknown** for any particular Future Me target until label audits exist.
- **Disposition:** Gate all future forecasting claims on a target/label specification. This is a research gate, not an implementation design.

### FA-02 — Completion and deadline events are safer targets than focus or capacity

- **Claim:** Near-term completion and deadline-miss events are more defensible first forecasting targets because they can be operationalized as outcomes; focus and usable capacity remain latent unless separately measured.
- **Status:** **Inferred**, strongly supported by the contracts.
- **Repository evidence:** The existing reflection flow can collect completed/partial/did-not-happen/skip. No current flow measures validated focus minutes or continuous capacity.
- **Independent evidence:** Time-to-event methods were developed for events whose time may be observed or censored, not for unnamed latent constructs ([Cox 1972](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1972.tb00899.x)). Personal-productivity research provides negative evidence against reducing the construct to app-use duration ([Kim et al. 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/understanding-personal-productivity-how-knowledge-workers-define-evaluate-and-reflect-on-their-productivity/)).
- **Negative evidence:** Completion can still be ambiguous (partial work, changed scope, abandoned tasks, checkbox hygiene). Deadline outcomes can be altered by scope negotiation or deadline changes.
- **Assumptions:** The task and success criterion are stable enough to resolve; scope/deadline changes are versioned rather than overwritten.
- **Failure conditions:** Treating partial as false without declaration, treating cancellation as failure, or using task completion as proof of focus/energy.
- **Transfer confidence:** **Medium-high** for choosing resolvable events; **medium** for any model until enough Future Me outcomes exist.
- **Disposition:** Prefer event targets for research evaluation; keep latent-state claims descriptive and unscored until measured.

### FA-03 — Rules establish constraint consequences, not behavioral probabilities

- **Claim:** Deterministic rules are appropriate for known calendar overlap, deadline ordering, freshness expiry, dependency invalidation, and arithmetic infeasibility; they do not by themselves estimate the probability of human behavior.
- **Status:** **Confirmed** by the MVP allowance for simple rules and by basic semantics.
- **Repository evidence:** The MVP explicitly permits deterministic rules and treats forecasting logic as provisional. `OVERLOADED` and `DISRUPTED` are system beliefs.
- **Independent evidence:** No statistical paper is needed to establish that a Boolean constraint evaluation and an empirically calibrated probability answer different questions.
- **Negative evidence:** A rule can be useful despite zero predictive validation, but its threshold embeds value judgments. A rule that maps “commitments > capacity” to overload is undefined until both sides use explicit units and provenance.
- **Assumptions:** Inputs are current and semantically valid; thresholds are identified as policy choices.
- **Failure conditions:** Printing `0.70` from a heuristic score, calling a rule calibrated, or inferring intent/mental state from a hard constraint.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for deterministic constraint checking; **low** for behavior prediction from such rules.
- **Disposition:** Use rules as auditable statements of assumptions and consequences; do not relabel rule scores as probabilities.

### FA-04 — Survival/hazard methods fit time-to-completion only after episode semantics exist

- **Claim:** Survival/hazard models are a plausible method for time until a defined completion or deadline event because they represent censoring and a time-varying event rate.
- **Status:** **Inferred / later empirical candidate**, not an MVP conclusion.
- **Mechanism evidence:** Cox's proportional-hazards work explicitly addresses censored failure times and covariates ([Cox 1972](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1972.tb00899.x)).
- **Future Me fit:** Potential targets include time from task activation to verified completion, or probability of remaining incomplete by deadline T.
- **Negative evidence:** Product feedback is likely informatively missing; tasks can be paused, abandoned, re-scoped, split, or completed outside observed systems. “Completion” and “censoring” may compete with cancellation or scope change. Proportional hazards may not hold.
- **Assumptions:** Start time, risk set, completion criterion, censoring reason, deadline version, and competing outcomes are recorded consistently.
- **Failure conditions:** Treating missing feedback as right-censoring without testing the missingness process; interpreting hazard as an individual causal mechanism; mixing unlike task types/horizons.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for the statistical mechanism; **low-to-medium** for Future Me until episode data quality is demonstrated.
- **Disposition:** Retain as a research comparison for resolved completion episodes; do not claim it solves focus, energy, or capacity measurement.

### FA-05 — Quantile regression is useful only for a defensible numeric outcome

- **Claim:** Quantile regression can estimate conditional quantiles of duration, time cost, or another numeric outcome and can expose asymmetric planning risk without assuming a Gaussian conditional distribution.
- **Status:** **Confirmed mechanism; unknown product value.**
- **Mechanism evidence:** Regression quantiles estimate conditional quantiles through asymmetric loss ([Koenker & Bassett 1978](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913643)). Proper evaluation uses quantile-consistent scoring such as pinball loss; coverage and width are also needed for intervals ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)).
- **Future Me fit:** Task duration or user-reported completed minutes can be candidates if their semantics are fixed. `P20/P50/P80 usable focus minutes` is not yet justified because “usable focus minute” is undefined and unobserved.
- **Negative evidence:** Quantiles can cross, sparse conditional regions are unstable, and ordinary quantile regression does not automatically handle censoring, regime changes, or coherent joint trajectories.
- **Assumptions:** Numeric outcomes are comparable; predictors precede the outcome; evaluation is time ordered; outcome missingness is addressed.
- **Failure conditions:** Calling quantiles “confidence intervals,” selecting a conservative quantile without a decision-loss rationale, or presenting quantiles for an invalid target.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for conditional quantile estimation; **low** for the prior report's focus/capacity examples; **medium** for well-defined duration after data collection.
- **Disposition:** Target validity precedes model choice. Quantile regression is not an “excellent MVP head” before a label audit.

### FA-06 — Bayesian/state-space models formalize latent dynamics but do not validate the latent state

- **Claim:** A Bayesian or state-space model can separate latent state dynamics from observation noise and update uncertainty sequentially.
- **Status:** **Confirmed mechanism; transfer to Future Me is unvalidated.**
- **Mechanism evidence:** Kalman's canonical filter derives recursive state and covariance updates under a specified linear stochastic system ([Kalman 1960](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3662552)). Bayesian online changepoint detection maintains a posterior over run length under a declared likelihood and hazard prior ([Adams & MacKay 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)).
- **Future Me fit:** A model could represent a carefully defined latent propensity only when the observation model connects it to repeated labels. Bayesian priors can regularize sparse histories and make assumptions visible.
- **Negative evidence:** Posterior precision can be false precision when the state, transition, or observation model is wrong. “Capacity,” “focus propensity,” and “deadline pressure” are not validated merely because they appear as `z_t`. Latent states may be non-identifiable, and a Bayesian credible interval is not evidence of empirical calibration.
- **Assumptions:** State meaning is fixed; observations measure it with a defensible model; priors and process noise are tested; missingness and interventions are represented.
- **Failure conditions:** State labels are chosen for narrative appeal, model assumptions are not checked, or filtered estimates are shown as facts.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for sequential estimation in well-specified systems; **low** for current Future Me latent constructs; **medium** only after repeated measurement validation.
- **Disposition:** Treat state-space modeling as a hypothesis to compare later, not as a current backbone or proof that hidden human states are knowable.

### FA-07 — Conformal prediction is a coverage wrapper, not a repair for invalid labels or drift

- **Claim:** Conformal methods can wrap a predictor to produce prediction sets/intervals with finite-sample marginal coverage under the applicable exchangeability/symmetry conditions.
- **Status:** **Confirmed with strict conditions.**
- **Mechanism evidence:** The correct introductory reference is Angelopoulos & Bates, arXiv `2107.07511`, not `0710.3742` ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). Beyond-exchangeability methods quantify or reduce coverage loss under particular forms of nonexchangeability and weighting; they do not restore universal conditional coverage ([Barber et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2276)).
- **Future Me fit:** Potentially useful after a base model, a stable target, and enough calibration examples exist.
- **Negative evidence:** One user's time series is dependent, nonstationary, and small. Regime shifts reduce effective sample size; recency weighting can widen or destabilize intervals. Marginal coverage can hide poor coverage for particular horizons, task types, or regimes.
- **Assumptions:** Calibration examples and future examples satisfy the chosen conformal method's conditions; target and conformity score remain stable.
- **Failure conditions:** Advertising “distribution-free 90% coverage” while ignoring time dependence/drift; interpreting a conformal interval as a Bayesian credible interval; wrapping an invalid focus/capacity label.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for the conditional theorem; **low** for current Future Me data; **medium** only after prospective coverage audits.
- **Disposition:** Exploratory later method. It cannot make the MVP's heuristic probabilities honest.

### FA-08 — Calibration is target-, horizon-, regime-, and missingness-specific

- **Claim:** Calibration is a relationship between prospectively issued forecasts and realized outcomes across cases; it is not a property of a single number or a generic model.
- **Status:** **Confirmed.**
- **Independent evidence:** Calibration and sharpness are distinct, with the goal of sharp forecasts subject to calibration ([Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x)). Calibration has levels beyond a single reliability diagram ([Van Calster et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2015.12.005)). Brier score decompositions distinguish reliability, resolution, and outcome uncertainty ([Murphy 1973](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0450%281973%29012%3C0595%3AANVPOT%3E2.0.CO%3B2)).
- **Future Me fit:** Binary events: Brier score versus a declared base-rate/no-change baseline, reliability with uncertainty, and calibration intercept/slope when sample size permits. Quantiles: pinball loss by quantile plus empirical coverage/width. Full distributions: CRPS when a coherent distribution is actually produced.
- **Negative evidence:** Small per-user samples make bins and slopes unstable. Selective feedback means the resolved set may not represent all forecasts. A globally calibrated model can be miscalibrated for a user, horizon, task type, or new regime.
- **Assumptions:** Forecasts are immutable; outcomes are prospectively defined; evaluation is chronological; unresolved/selectively observed cases are reported.
- **Failure conditions:** Random train/test splits that leak future regimes, recalibrating and evaluating on the same cases, showing probability language before prospective evidence, or optimizing only AUROC/accuracy.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for principles and scores; **low-to-medium** for estimates from early Future Me data.
- **Disposition:** Before evidence exists, label outputs as deterministic assessments or unvalidated estimates—not calibrated probability bands. `20/50/80 percent` are numerical probabilities/quantiles, not harmless coarse language.

### FA-09 — Concept drift is not the same as a changed calendar fact

- **Claim:** Concept drift means the predictive relationship/distribution changes over time; a moved deadline or added meeting is an observed covariate/context change, not automatically concept drift.
- **Status:** **Confirmed distinction.**
- **Independent evidence:** The concept-drift literature distinguishes detection, understanding, and adaptation and warns that stale models can degrade under changing stream distributions ([Lu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/TKDE.2018.2876857)).
- **Future Me fit:** Direct context changes should first invalidate dependent assumptions deterministically. Statistical drift monitoring becomes relevant only after a model emits repeated predictions and receives delayed resolved labels.
- **Negative evidence:** Seasonality, temporary shocks, sensor changes, policy changes, missing feedback, and outcome-definition changes can mimic drift. A detector identifies statistical change, not its cause or user meaning.
- **Assumptions:** The monitored series (features, residuals, losses, coverage) has stable semantics and adequate frequency.
- **Failure conditions:** Running a drift detector on raw heterogeneous events, auto-rewriting personal context after an alarm, or treating an alarm as evidence of changed intent.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** for the distinction; **low** for present detection because Future Me has no validated forecast-error stream.
- **Disposition:** Current needs are freshness/conflict checks. Statistical drift detection is deferred until there are sequential forecasts and outcomes.

### FA-10 — Changepoint methods answer different questions

- **Claim:** Bayesian online detection, adaptive-window drift detection, and retrospective segmentation should not be treated as interchangeable.
- **Status:** **Confirmed mechanism comparison.**
- **Method evidence:**
  - Bayesian online changepoint detection estimates a posterior distribution over run length and needs a likelihood plus hazard prior ([Adams & MacKay 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)).
  - ADWIN changes the effective window after statistically detected distribution changes in a data stream and provides guarantees in its formal setting ([Bifet & Gavaldà 2007](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611972771.42)).
  - PELT is an exact retrospective multiple-changepoint segmentation method under its penalized-cost conditions, not an online user-state oracle ([Killick, Fearnhead & Eckley 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2012.737745)).
- **Future Me fit:** BOCPD could later express uncertainty over abrupt regime boundaries; adaptive windows could monitor a scalar loss or calibration residual stream; PELT could audit historical episodes offline.
- **Negative evidence:** None handles gradual, recurring, seasonal, or label-delayed change automatically. Sparse irregular observations undermine prompt detection and false-alarm control.
- **Assumptions:** A homogeneous monitored statistic, declared change model/cost, sufficient observations, and an action policy that tolerates false alarms.
- **Failure conditions:** Change alarms mutate ground truth; the monitored series changes because instrumentation changed; repeated testing is ignored; detection delay is unacceptable.
- **Transfer confidence:** **Medium** for later diagnostics; **low** for current personal-state inference.
- **Disposition:** Compare only after replayable labeled sequences exist. Until then, store timestamps and preserve old/new claims.

### FA-11 — Feedback selection is part of the measurement model

- **Claim:** User feedback cannot be treated as an unbiased random sample of all episodes.
- **Status:** **Confirmed risk; magnitude unknown for Future Me.**
- **Independent evidence:** EMA compliance varies by time and person, and systematic nonresponse can create data not missing at random ([Courvoisier et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026733)). Repeated prompts also create burden and potential reactivity.
- **Future Me fit:** Users may answer successful, memorable, or low-burden episodes more often. Dismissed prompts may correlate with overload, interruption, or low energy—the exact outcomes of interest.
- **Negative evidence:** The product currently has no data to estimate the direction or size of this bias.
- **Assumptions:** Prompt issuance, display, dismissal, snooze, answer latency, skip, and no-response are distinguishable without treating silence as consent.
- **Failure conditions:** Training only on respondents without a selection audit; calling user reports “gold labels”; increasing prompt frequency solely to improve sample size.
- **Transfer confidence:** **High** that selective missingness is possible; **unknown** magnitude and correction feasibility in Future Me.
- **Disposition:** Report resolved-case rate and response selection alongside forecast scores. Unknown remains unknown.

## 5. Method-family comparison

| Family | Appropriate target | Handles uncertainty as | Data/label requirement | Calibration/evaluation | Main failure mode in Future Me | Pass A verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deterministic rules/constraints** | Overlap, slack, ordering, changed deadline, freshness, arithmetic infeasibility | Explicit unknown inputs, intervals, policy thresholds—not learned probability | Current structured plans/observations | Truth table, scenario tests, false-positive review; probability calibration is inapplicable unless empirically mapped | Policy score masquerades as probability or mental-state truth | **Use for clearly defined facts/constraints; not behavior forecasting** |
| **Survival/hazard** | Time to completion; probability still incomplete by T; deadline event with censoring | Survival curve/hazard conditional on covariates | Repeated episode starts, outcome times, censoring/competing-event reasons | Time-ordered survival calibration and proper survival scores; compare simple Kaplan–Meier/base-rate-style baselines where appropriate | Informative missingness, task re-scoping, violated proportional hazards, ambiguous risk set | **Plausible later comparison for completion/deadline episodes** |
| **Quantile regression** | Duration, time cost, completed minutes, other numeric outcome | Conditional quantiles | Repeated comparable numeric outcomes | Pinball loss per quantile; empirical interval coverage and width; temporal backtest | Invalid “focus minutes” target, quantile crossing, sparse tails, unhandled censoring/drift | **Good mechanism only after target validity** |
| **Bayesian/state-space** | A validated dynamic latent state plus noisy repeated measurements | Posterior/predictive distributions; process and observation noise | Sequential observations and an identifiable observation model; priors | Posterior predictive checks plus prospective empirical calibration | A narrative latent variable acquires false legitimacy; misspecified dynamics create confident error | **Research hypothesis, not current ground truth or required backbone** |
| **Conformal** | Prediction set/interval around an existing model's observable target | Empirical rank/quantile coverage under stated conditions | Calibration cases representative under the selected method | Coverage and width overall and by horizon/regime; monitor coverage drift | Exchangeability failure, tiny effective N, only marginal coverage, invalid base target | **Deferred until labels and a base model exist** |
| **Online changepoint (BOCPD)** | Abrupt change in a homogeneous sequential process | Posterior over run length/change | Frequent sequential observations and likelihood/hazard specification | False alarms, delay, predictive score before/after alarms | Wrong hazard/likelihood; gradual/seasonal changes; sparse irregular data | **Later diagnostic, not current context truth** |
| **Adaptive-window drift (e.g., ADWIN)** | Shift in scalar error/statistic stream | Statistical alarm and changing reference window | Dense stream with stable semantics; labels if monitoring errors | False-positive/negative rate and detection delay | Sensor/policy changes mistaken for user change; repeated-test burden | **Later monitor on losses/residuals, not raw life events** |
| **Retrospective changepoint (e.g., PELT)** | Offline segmentation of a historical scalar series | Penalized multiple-change segmentation | Sufficient historical series and a declared cost/penalty | Replay against labeled/simulated changes; sensitivity to penalty | Post-hoc story mistaken for online evidence or cause | **Useful for audit experiments only** |

No family repairs an undefined target. Model sophistication can reduce estimation error while increasing confidence in the wrong construct.

## 6. Calibration and uncertainty audit

### 6.1 What can be said now

- Deterministic rules may report their inputs, assumptions, and whether a constraint is satisfied.
- Heuristic scores may be called scores or unvalidated risk bands if thresholds are explicitly policy-defined.
- They must not be called calibrated probabilities.
- A range derived solely from user-entered best/likely/worst estimates is a scenario range, not an empirically calibrated predictive interval.
- Unknown target validity and unknown model performance are separate from ordinary outcome randomness.

### 6.2 Minimum prospective evaluation contract

This is an audit criterion, not an implementation specification. A future study would need, at minimum:

1. An immutable forecast timestamp and training cutoff.
2. One operational target and one resolution rule per evaluation stream.
3. A declared horizon and comparison set.
4. Outcome states that include true, false, partial (when meaningful), expired, changed-definition, skipped, and not observed.
5. Baselines available at forecast time: no-change/last-known state, empirical base rate, simple recent mean, and deterministic clock-time feasibility where relevant.
6. Time-ordered or prequential evaluation; never random splitting across future regime changes.
7. Binary forecasts: Brier score and reliability diagnostics with uncertainty, plus calibration intercept/slope only when sample size supports them.
8. Quantiles: pinball loss by quantile, coverage, and width. Full distributions: CRPS only if the forecast is a coherent distribution.
9. Separate reporting by target, horizon, task/commitment type, user maturity, and detected regime when sample size permits; otherwise say the slice is unknown.
10. Resolution rate, response rate, answer latency, skip/dismissal rate, and sensitivity to plausible missing-outcome mechanisms.
11. Sharpness/informativeness only after calibration; decision utility only after forecasting performance and the action policy are separately defined.

There is no defensible universal sample-size threshold in the reviewed evidence. Early reliability diagrams can be descriptive but unstable. Release thresholds must be predeclared for a specific target and decision cost; inventing a generic “enough cases” number would be another overclaim.

### 6.3 Uncertainty types that must remain separate

- **Aleatoric/outcome uncertainty:** even with complete context, the future varies.
- **Parameter/model uncertainty:** little history, uncertain coefficients, or competing model forms.
- **Measurement uncertainty:** labels/proxies do not perfectly measure the target.
- **Target ambiguity:** the product has not defined what “focus,” “capacity,” or “overload” means. This cannot be fixed by wider intervals.
- **Distribution shift/drift:** future cases may not follow the historical relationship.
- **Decision uncertainty:** the preferred action changes with values, loss asymmetry, or reversibility even if the forecast is stable.

The prior report correctly separates several uncertainty types, but then often gives precise `P20/P50/P80 usable focus` examples before target ambiguity and label quality are resolved. That precision is illustrative, not evidence.

## 7. Concept drift audit

Future Me should distinguish four phenomena:

1. **Observed context change:** deadline moved, commitment added, calendar event cancelled. Resolve directly and invalidate dependent claims.
2. **Covariate shift:** the distribution of inputs changes—for example, a new semester changes calendar load—but the outcome relationship may or may not change.
3. **Concept drift:** the relationship between inputs and the defined target changes—for example, the same free-time pattern no longer predicts block completion.
4. **Instrumentation/label drift:** connector semantics, prompts, outcome definitions, or response behavior change. This can look like user drift but is a measurement-system change.

Current contracts justify freshness and conflict handling for item 1. Items 2–4 require a stream of versioned forecasts, features, policies, and resolved outcomes. Before that stream exists, “concept drift detection” is not measurable.

Safe interpretation of a later alarm would be: “the recent error/coverage stream is inconsistent with the reference regime under method M.” It would not be: “the user's motivation changed.” A statistical alarm should lower reliance, widen uncertainty where justified, or request review; it cannot silently rewrite a user claim.

## 10. Assumptions and failure conditions across the program

### Assumptions that must be tested, not merely documented

- Target semantics remain stable across users and time.
- Feedback responses are sufficiently representative, or selection bias can be bounded.
- User corrections and deadline/scope changes are versioned.
- Features available at inference time are also available in replay without leakage.
- A “partial” outcome has a defined treatment appropriate to the target.
- Cross-user pooling, if ever studied, does not erase meaningful heterogeneity and has explicit consent/governance.
- The action taken after a forecast does not make evaluation uninterpretable; intervention and forecast performance must be separated.
- Forecast frequency does not create many correlated pseudo-samples from one episode.

### Stop conditions for a forecasting claim

Abstain from probability language or stop scoring the stream when:

- the target definition changes without a new version;
- outcome resolution falls selectively or cannot be distinguished from silence;
- calibration/coverage degrades outside predeclared tolerance;
- a source or prompt changes semantics;
- a drift alarm cannot be separated from instrumentation change;
- the model does not beat a simple baseline on prospective data;
- intervals become too wide to change a decision;
- the user-facing action causes more prompt burden/correction than measured benefit; or
- the product starts optimizing a proxy that users do not endorse as their outcome.

## 12. Pass A conclusion

Future Me currently has a defensible basis for **deterministic plan/constraint checks**, **observed context-change handling**, and a future **shadow evaluation of narrowly defined completion or deadline events**. It does not yet have ground truth for usable capacity, focus minutes, energy, or generic overload. Those terms must be split into observable events, user-reported subjective states, and explicit proxies before any model is judged.

Rules, survival/hazard models, quantile regression, Bayesian/state-space models, conformal methods, and drift detectors answer different questions. None is a final choice from this pass. The decisive bottleneck is measurement: target definition, outcome resolution, selective missingness, versioned provenance, and chronological evaluation. Until those exist, numerical probability or quantile examples are hypotheses, not calibrated product claims.
